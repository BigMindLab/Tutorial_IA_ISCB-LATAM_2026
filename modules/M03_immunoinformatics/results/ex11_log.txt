[question 2] Is it a membrane protein, and which way round? (slide 19)
  UniProt annotates 16 transmembrane beta strands.
  >fhaC | BETA  length=584
  label counts: {'S': 32, 'P': 272, 'B': 138, 'O': 142}
  predicted beta strands: 16
  UniProt beta strands  : 16
  raw per residue agreement: 47.3%
  That number looks poor, and it is misleading. The reference
  marks every non strand residue as 'O' because UniProt does not
  label inside and outside, so every 'P' DeepTMHMM predicts is
  counted as a mismatch even when it is right.
  agreement on the strand or not strand question: 88.4%
  That is the comparison the reference can actually support.

[why this matters downstream]
  The first 30 residues are cut off during secretion, so they are not
  part of the mature protein. Any class I or class II candidate that
  falls entirely inside 1-30 should be dropped before ranking.
    9-mers fully inside the signal peptide: 22

QUESTIONS TO DISCUSS

  1. SignalP gives a signal peptide type and a cleavage site. Neither
     says anything about antigenicity. What does each one actually
     constrain in the rest of this workflow?
  2. The reference here is curated annotation, much of it from a solved
     structure. If a predictor disagrees with it, is the predictor wrong?
  3. DeepTMHMM labels every residue. SignalP labels only the N terminus.
     Why does that difference matter when you chain them together?
  4. fhaC is an extracellular bacterial protein. Which MHC class is the
     natural route for it, and what does that imply for exercises that
     only predict class I? (Look back at slide 20.)
